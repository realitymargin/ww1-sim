How it works

Every simulated war runs from August 1914 in half-year turns through 40 real turning points in their real order. At each one the computer rolls a die against an odd: does it go the way it really went, or the other way? Between the turning points the war is carried by real data: each power's 1913 economy and wartime output (Broadberry & Harrison 2005), the U-boat sinkings and Britain's fleet month by month (Fayle 1924), and the Americans arriving (Ayres 1919). Each side's will to fight drains every half-year; whoever reaches zero first asks for terms.

Every war has its own fixed dice (seed 19140728), so a run is repeatable: the same settings always give the same numbers, on any computer. The experiments use the model's experiment seed (1918) and 100,000 wars, as in the video.

Questions

Is this really the model from the video?

Yes. The code is the video's model, version 3.1, locked on 31 October 2026. The browser runs the same file with only its Node file import removed.

Where do the odds come from?

They are judgements, written down openly with their reasons and sources on the sources page. The three columns follow historians who think the odds were against the Central Powers, the middle (the video), and those who think they were better.

What counts as a win?

The Central Powers win when the Western Allies are forced to ask for terms. If both sides are exhausted, the war ends in a compromise peace; some wars are still going in 1920.

Does it send my settings anywhere?

No. Everything runs in your browser. The page counts visits and RUN presses with GoatCounter, which uses no cookies and collects no personal data; your settings and results are never sent. A shared link only carries the settings themselves.
